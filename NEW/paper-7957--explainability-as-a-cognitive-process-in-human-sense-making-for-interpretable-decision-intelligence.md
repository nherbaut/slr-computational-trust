  title: Explainability as a Cognitive Process in Human Sense-Making for Interpretable Decision Intelligence
  authors: L. B. Muralidhar, N. Sathyanarayana, H. R. Swapna, S. Madhu, K. P. Sheeba
  abstract: This book chapter reframes explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) as a cognitive and sense-making process rather
    than algorithmic transparency. It studies how people perceive, analyse, and act on AI-generated outputs under ambiguity,
    bounded rationality, and bias using cognitive science, neuroscience, philosophy, and decision intelligence. The chapter
    analyses XAI paradigms, emphasises cognitive misalignments, and reframes decision intelligence as a human–AI socio-cognitive
    system focused on judgement quality rather than prediction accuracy. The Cognitive Explainability–Sense-Making Framework
    (CESF) links explanations to mental models, trust calibration, and decision outcomes and provides empirical validation.
    The chapter explores ethical, organisational, and governance effects utilising empirical evidence, case studies, cross-cultural
    views, and policy analysis. It concludes with future research and advocates cognitively aligned, context-sensitive explainability
    for ethical, trustworthy, and successful AI-mediated decision-making.
