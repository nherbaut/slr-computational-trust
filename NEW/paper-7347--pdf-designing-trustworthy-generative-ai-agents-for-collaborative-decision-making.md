  title: '[PDF] Designing Trustworthy Generative AI Agents for Collaborative Decision Making'
  authors: Joséphine Mélot-Chesnel
  abstract: As generative AI (genAI) becomes increasingly integrated into decision-making environments, designing agents that
    users can trust is both a technical and ethical imperative. From a human-centered design perspective, understanding how
    these agents can shape human behavior and task performance is critical. During this project, a systematic literature review
    mapping the current landscape of trust measurement and calibration in collaborative genAI systems will be followed by
    three empirical user-studies in which participants will have to collaborate with a conversational agent (or textual genAI)
    during decision-making tasks. Environmental factors (e.g., task difficulty and collaboration setting) and agent characteristics
    (e.g., display of uncertainty and perceived competence) will be manipulated to better understand how they influence trust
    as an attitude and trusting use as observable behaviors (including, but not limited to, reliance, verification, or delegation).
    By drawing on insights from psychology, artificial intelligence, human–agent interaction, and ethics, and integrating
    system performance metrics (like explanation fidelity) with human-centered measures (such as trust behavior or cognitive
    load) and overall collaboration performance, the project aims to advance human-centered AI design and deepen understanding
    of how trust is formed, maintained, and calibrated to mitigate both under-trust and over-reliance in human–genAI decision-making
    environments.
